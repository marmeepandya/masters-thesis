For the paper, I'm using People Data Labs' free company dataset as an open, publishable stand-in for Istari's proprietary GOI corpus. It's a CC BY 4.0-licensed, quarterly-updated export of about 35.8 million companies worldwide, sourced from public web and LinkedIn signals, with structured fields per company: name, website domain, industry classification, employee-size bracket, founding year, and location (country/region/locality). Around 83% of records have an industry label and 66% have a website, reasonably complete given the scale. The main gap versus GOI is that it has no free-text description field, only structured attributes, so I'm generating a templated pseudo-description per company from those fields to give the retrieval pipeline something to embed, since GOI's AI-generated summaries are what the whole vocabulary-mismatch story in the thesis depends on. The goal is to reproduce the thesis's pipeline and results on this public dataset so the paper doesn't depend on Istari's non-redistributable data."
